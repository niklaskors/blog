## Response to Bas Heijne on Identity

In response to the [NRC article](https://archive.is/iWlMJ) on identity based on the book by Alexander Douglas called:
"Against Identity". Identity, according to Douglas, is seen as an illusion which makes us rigid and unbearable for
others. According to the article, Douglas's thought is influenced by the work of the following thinkers:

- René Girard
- Spinoza
- Zhuangzi

Besides the book by Douglas, the article also references several others. I will not make the distinction between the
different books and articles but respond to the article as a whole. Additionally, I am not sure if the article is a good
representation of the book or Alexander Douglas's thought. I will respond to the article as it is and have added some
excerpts from the article below with my responses to them.

### Excerpts

> Steeds maar bezig zijn te ontdekken wie je bent, zonde van je tijd.

This statement goes against what is, in my opinion, one of the most important things in life. Like Socrates said: "The
unexamined life is not worth living." I would also argue that the very conclusions drawn by the authors are the result
of a deep examination of the self. If you dig long enough, you will eventually find that the ego is not the true self
and accordingly find that this identity is an illusion. Besides, I also believe that one first needs to be aware of the
ego before one can transcend it. And to transcend it does not mean to get rid of it; just bringing it into awareness and
its associated limitations should be enough.

> Vanuit onszelf zijn we niks bepaalds, alles wat we van binnen denken te vinden, vinden we in werkelijkheid buiten
> onszelf. Anders gezegd: we doen niet, we doen na.

I partially agree with this statement, but it depends heavily on the context in which it is made. If the context is the
ego, then yes: everything the ego takes itself to be, or knows about other things, is a representation of what exists
outside of it. In other words, these are shallow copies of things-in-themselves. This connects to another statement in
the article: "Also, I am as shallow as a puddle." However, to claim that we merely imitate everything from the outside
world would deny access to the inner world. Additionally, I believe that both the within and the without are infinite
and that we can find the infinite both within and without. Now, to say that we imitate from an infinite source makes the
original statement sound less dismissive. I would also like to rephrase 'imitation' into 'continuation' and change it
into something like: "We are continuations from an arbitrary starting point on the infinite."

> De waarden en overtuigingen die deel uitmaken van onze „identiteit” mogen niet bevraagd worden, want dan komt ons
> „wezen” in gevaar. Daarom kunnen we elkaar de hersens inslaan vanwege betekenisloze symbolen, daarom willen we in de
> debatten niet overtuigen maar domineren, daarom houden we vast aan denkbeelden en meningen wanneer de feiten ons
> domweg ongelijk geven.

I am sure that identity and the ego are seen as the same thing here. Therefore, I do agree with this statement and the
dark side that the ego can bring.

> Waarom wil een mens iemand uit één stuk zijn? Aan de grondslag van de hang naar identiteit, betoogt Douglas, ligt een
> metafysisch streven. De ander die wij als voorbeeld nemen lijkt te hebben wat wijzelf zo jammerlijk missen – een
> onvervreemdbare kern, een vaste eigenheid, die ons in staat stelt de onbegrijpelijke wirwar in onszelf te overstijgen.

Yes, I think this is a very important statement. The ego wants to be whole and complete, but it is inherently incomplete
and therefore fragmented. I really like that the author points to the metaphysical roots of the problem. Probably the
term religion is purposely avoided here, but I would argue that this is one of the problems that religion tries to
solve. You can clearly see the ongoing process of working with this problem in Christianity with the ritual of
communion.
