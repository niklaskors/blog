## Religion and Nothingness

Thanks to John Vervaeke's illuminating series called
[Awakening from the Meaning Crisis](https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PLND1JCRq8Vuh3f0P5qjrSdb5eC1ZfZwWJ&si=E-dNzdXBrK0dpreO)
I was first introduced to the thinkers of the Japanese Kyoto School. From what I have understood from Vervaeke and
various opinions on the internet this is not an easy read and I can confirm that. I am currently making my way through
this book for a second time. My first attempt was a few years ago and I did not get very far. I knew however that this
was a very important book and that it had the potential to dissolve some of the boundaries between eastern and western
thought. During my first attempt it was clear that a lot of references the book was making were lost on me and that I
was lacking the philosophical background to fully grasp the concepts. I am in no position to say that I do have that
background now, but I am making a lot more connections to other works I have been reading and am noticing some common
grounds.

### Breaking through the ego and finding a new Ground

### Some of Nishitani's thoughts in my own words

The ego, the Great prison. The Cartesian vault. The place that considers itself to be independent and free. The place
that calls itself the self. Yet, to call it a place or consider it to be as such, means it is something different from
the Self. A call always needs a caller. This egoic self represents a collection of references to the Self but is
something inherently different. It is the reference but not the thing referred to. Holding on to both as being real,
necessarily produces a kind of dualism where the one who considers is independent from the considered. We should
realize, both appropriate and .. that both the Self and the egoic self are one and the same thing. There can only be the
one Self.

### Quotes

"This is the omnipresence of God in all things that have their being as a creatio ex nihilo. It follows that this
omnipresence can be said to represent for man the dynamic motif of the transposition of absolute negation and absolute
affirmation. To entrust the self to this motif, to let oneself be driven by it so as to die to the self and live in God,
is what constitutes faith. The advent of Christ can then be seen as the corporeal apparition to man of this motif of
conversion as it occurs within God himself."

"The love of Christ being at one and the same time a sword that kills man and a sword that gives man life."
