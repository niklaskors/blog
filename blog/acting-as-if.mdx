## Acting As-If

Acting as if a underlying presupposition were actually true can sometimes have beneficial outcomes.

**Underlying Beliefs About Human Nature**

In exploring human nature, contrasting theories propose different inherent predispositions:

- **Theory A** suggests people are fundamentally self-serving and would revert to selfish, hostile, and destructive
  behaviours without societal controls.
- **Theory B** posits that individuals have no innate nature but are shaped solely by genetics and experiences,
  essentially blank slates.
- **Theory C** proposes a natural predisposition towards collaboration, constructive behaviour, and trustworthiness,
  especially under supportive conditions that facilitate positive change.

While these theories cannot be definitively proven, embracing them has significant consequences. In management theory,
Douglas McGregor differentiated between "Theories X and Y." Theory X assumes workers are lazy and require coercion,
while Theory Y believes workers are creative and self-motivated, needing a supportive environment to excel. Successful
businesses prefer Theory Y to inspire collaboration and commitment.
